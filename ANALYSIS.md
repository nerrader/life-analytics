## Methodology

For the analysis part, I've decided to split it into five phases, to provide structure on when I should collect data, answer questions, conduct experiments to try and prove causality, and when I can draw conclusions from data.

### Phase 1

The first phase will just be about collecting data. I will not try and draw conclusions and change/modify the question list, and I also won't change my behavior. I'll just live my life as usual.

The goal for this phase is just to collect enough data to move on to the next phase.

### Phase 2

This phase will be about exploring the collected data in the first phase and finding interesting topics or relationships in the data that I could potentially add in my questions list to further investigate later on in either phase 3 or 4.

### Phase 3

This phase will be about answering questions that can be answered using observational data, like questions that ask about correlations.

Then, I will draw conclusions when there is enough data to support the finding. These will only show associations, not causations.

### Phase 4

This phase will be about changing my behavior as an experiment to try and answer some questions that involve inferring causality. As these types of questions need more controlled environments to answer.

Remember kids: **correlation does not equal causation**.

### Phase 5

This phase aims to conclude all the previous answers, and convert them into actionable insights on what I could do to improve my life.

Though while I did say I was going to turn data into questions later, I would like to set up some questions I want to answer now, like, an initial questions list that I will modify in the second phase let's say:

- Is mood positively or negatively associated to productivity?
- Does taking a nap seem to affect that day's mood or productivity in a positive way?
- Does sleep duration correlate with sleep quality?
- Does sleep quality tend increase on the weekends?
- Does my perceived productivity increase on the weekends?
- Does sleep quality seem to affect the day's mood, productivity, or even stress?
- Does my energy seem to affect productivity or mood?
- What activities tend to increase or decrease my energy?
- Does energy increase correlate with higher activity enjoyment, and vice versa?

And here are the lists of questions I want to test in the 4th phase:

- Does intentionally taking more breaks improve my productivity, mood, or stress?
- Does getting a more consistent amount of sleep improve my mood, productivity, or stress?
- Does reducing time spent idle actually increase my productivity?

Though this list may change later on depending on the second phase's data exploration findings.
